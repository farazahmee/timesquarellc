Most B2B teams don't lose time to hard problems. They lose it to small, repetitive
tasks that quietly repeat hundreds of times a month. AI automation is most valuable
exactly there — in the gaps between your tools.

## Where the hours actually hide

When we audit a new client's workflows, the same time sinks show up again and again:

- **Manual data entry** between a CRM, a spreadsheet, and an invoicing tool.
- **Copy-paste reporting** that someone rebuilds by hand every Monday.
- **Inbox triage** — reading, categorising, and routing routine requests.
- **Follow-up chasing** that depends on a person remembering to send it.

Individually, each takes only a few minutes. Across a team, across a month, they add
up to the equivalent of a full working week — often more.

## What "20 hours" looks like in practice

A typical mid-size team we work with automates three flows first:

1. **Lead intake** — new form submissions are enriched, scored, and pushed into the
   CRM with a drafted first reply. *~6 hours/week.*
2. **Reporting** — dashboards refresh themselves and a written summary lands in
   Slack every Monday morning. *~5 hours/week.*
3. **Operational handoffs** — approvals, reminders, and status updates move between
   systems without anyone copying data by hand. *~9 hours/week.*

That's roughly 20 hours returned to the team — not by working faster, but by not
doing the work at all.

## How to find your own 20 hours

Ask your team one question: *"What did you do this week that a checklist could have
done for you?"* The answers are your automation backlog.
